MEDLINEPLUS - ASTHMA MANAGEMENT AND ENVIRONMENTAL CONTROLS

1. WHAT IS ASTHMA?
Asthma is a chronic disease that affects the airways in your lungs. The airways are tubes that carry air into and out of your lungs. If you have asthma, the inner walls of your airways become swollen and inflamed. During an asthma attack, the muscles around the airways tighten, narrowing the air passages.

2. COMMON TRIGGERS
Asthma attacks can be triggered by various environmental factors:
- Airborne substances: Pollen, dust mites, mold spores, pet dander, or cockroach waste particles.
- Respiratory infections: Such as the common cold or flu.
- Environmental irritants: Tobacco smoke, air pollution, chemical fumes, and strong odors.
- Physical exertion: Exercise-induced bronchospasm, especially in cold, dry air.
- Weather: Cold air or sudden temperature changes.

3. RECOGNIZING ASTHMA SYMPTOMS
- Shortness of breath
- Chest tightness or pain
- Wheezing when exhaling (a common sign of asthma in children)
- Trouble sleeping caused by shortness of breath, coughing, or wheezing
- Coughing or wheezing attacks that are worsened by a respiratory virus

4. DAILY MANAGEMENT & PREVENTION TIPS
- Identify and avoid specific asthma triggers.
- Monitor your breathing and recognize early warning signs of flare-ups.
- Keep living areas clean, dust-free, and well-ventilated.
- Use air conditioning or HEPA filters to reduce indoor allergen levels.
- Avoid exposure to secondhand tobacco smoke and air pollutants.
