CENTERS FOR DISEASE CONTROL AND PREVENTION (CDC) - INFLUENZA (FLU) GUIDELINES

1. UNDERSTANDING INFLUENZA
Influenza (flu) is a contagious respiratory illness caused by influenza viruses that infect the nose, throat, and sometimes the lungs. It can cause mild to severe illness, and at times can lead to death.

2. FLU SYMPTOMS VS COMMON COLD
Flu symptoms usually come on suddenly. People who have flu often feel some or all of these symptoms:
- Fever or feeling feverish/chills (not everyone with flu has a fever)
- Cough and sore throat
- Runny or stuffy nose
- Muscle or body aches, and headaches
- Fatigue (tiredness)
- Some people may have vomiting and diarrhea, though this is more common in children than adults.

3. HIGH-RISK POPULATIONS
Anyone can get sick with flu, but certain people are at higher risk of developing serious flu-related complications:
- Adults 65 years of age and older
- People of any age with certain chronic medical conditions (such as asthma, diabetes, or heart disease)
- Pregnant individuals
- Children younger than 5 years old (especially under 2 years)

4. HOME CARE & SUPPORTIVE MEASURES
For mild flu symptoms:
- Stay home and get plenty of rest.
- Drink clear fluids (water, broth, herbal tea, electrolyte solutions) to prevent dehydration.
- Cover your mouth and nose when coughing or sneezing.
- Wash your hands frequently with soap and water for at least 20 seconds.
- Avoid close contact with family members to prevent spreading the infection.
